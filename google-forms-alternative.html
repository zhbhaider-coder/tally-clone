Google Forms alternative: a free form builder with more design and logic

Google Forms is a solid, free tool — and for a quick internal poll, it may be all you need. But when the form represents your brand, needs to feel like part of your website, or has to adapt to each respondent, its limits start to show. Here's an honest comparison, and what a different kind of builder does instead.

Where Google Forms falls short

Branding and design

Google Forms offers a header image, a theme color, and a choice of fonts — and that's about it. Every form looks unmistakably like a Google Form, with Google's layout and styling. If the form sits on your website or goes to your customers, that generic look works against you.

Logic

Google Forms supports section-based branching: based on an answer, the respondent jumps to a different section. It works, but it's coarse — you branch whole sections, not individual questions, and you can't do calculations, pipe an earlier answer into a later question, or show and hide single blocks.

Payments and advanced blocks

Google Forms has no built-in way to collect payments, signatures, or file uploads beyond Google Drive, and no calculator-style fields. For orders, bookings, applications, and contracts, you end up bolting on add-ons or a second tool.

The Google account requirement

Building a form needs a Google account, and responses naturally live in Google Sheets. That's convenient inside Google Workspace — and a lock-in everywhere else.

What this builder does differently

  • An editor that works like a document. Type your questions on a blank page and insert blocks as you go, the way you would in Notion — no dragging fields around a canvas.
  • Design that matches your brand. Change colors, fonts, and button styles, or start from a theme, so the form looks like it belongs on your site.
  • Logic that adapts per question. Show or hide individual blocks, pipe answers into later questions, and calculate scores or prices as the respondent types.
  • No account required. Build, publish, and share without signing up — and respondents never need an account either.
  • Everything in one place. Responses land in a built-in inbox with CSV export, instead of living in a spreadsheet you have to remember to open.

Switching from Google Forms

There is no automatic importer — rebuilding is the honest path, and it is fast. Open the editor, copy your questions across one by one, and pick the matching question type for each. Most forms take under ten minutes. If your form is a survey, start from a survey template instead and adjust the wording; the survey maker guide explains which question types fit which questions. Your old Google Form can stay live while you test the new one — switch the link only when you're happy.

Questions & answers

Is Google Forms free?

Yes. Google Forms is free with a Google account, and responses can be sent to Google Sheets. Its limits are in design, branding, and logic — not price.

What does this builder do that Google Forms does not?

A Notion-style editor that works like a document, deeper design control (colors, fonts, layouts), conditional logic, answer piping, calculations, payment collection, and branding removal on paid plans — without needing a Google account.

Can I use it without a Google account?

Yes. You can build and publish a form with no signup at all, and respondents do not need any account to fill it in.

Is switching hard?

No. Rebuild your form in the editor — most forms take a few minutes — or start from a matching template and adjust the wording.
